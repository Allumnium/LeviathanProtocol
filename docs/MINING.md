# Asteroids and mining — steps 41–43, 45–46

Asteroids are neutral actors in the existing weapon/command/visibility system. Either side can damage them. Clicking a visible rock/hostile orders armed player hulls to focus it, with an amber target marker; clicking empty battlefield clears the orders. Dragging continues to pan, and GUI/radar clicks never issue an attack. Real pointer tests cover visible targeting and hidden-space rejection. Ordinary victory excludes rocks; the existing optional neutral-cleanup checkbox includes them. No new mandatory clearing objective was added.

The four classes have mass 8/4/2/1, radius `8 * sqrt(mass)` and HP `12 * mass`. They drift on the fixed simulation clock. Destruction splits a larger rock into two half-mass children, with deterministic separation velocities and 0.5 seconds of collision grace. A mass-one rock becomes terminal debris/pickup. An impact deals `6 * mass` to the contacted hull/component and removes one quarter of the rock's maximum HP. Bodies separate, and each rock/ship pair has a 0.75-second cooldown. Both factions obey these rules. Projectiles use swept segment obstruction; beams also strike an intervening rock. Finishing-camera predictions exclude obstructed shots.

There are at most 48 initial roots and 96 living rocks, separate from the ship/craft budget. A fracture without room stays an inert, untargetable, non-colliding parent until room opens. The lineage validator checks parent identities, class changes, exact child mineral partitions, terminal claims and cumulative root payments. Resolved parents are never additional mineral budgets.

New campaigns place six-root sparse fields on selected ordinary routes. Half the initial roots are empty. Fields consume 15% of the node's frozen threat allocation, reducing the alien allocation without inspecting the player. The map announces Asteroids before departure. Field generation avoids the initial formation areas. Existing frozen encounters remain unchanged. Dense fields and optional hazardous travel have not been activated.

Foundation Scan discovers contents without permanent moving-rock tracking. Basic Extraction follows Foundation directly: optional Mineral Spectroscopy is not a prerequisite. It grants Mining Laser/Extraction Drill and Offense/Defense recovery. Deep Extraction grants Ore Processor/Seismic Cutter and Tech/Civic recovery. All four pieces use normal general designs, physical component damage and paid construction. Their current art is the established material-cell placeholder system; the core recognition/art pass remains step 66.

Mining Laser deals 12 rock DPS at range 300; Drill deals 24 at range 45. Tool range is measured from the actual rotated component to the rock surface. Both deal one quarter of their rock DPS to ships. First damage snapshots the root's material permissions and eligible deployed equipment: ordinary weapons 25%, Laser/Cutter 75%, Drill 100%, plus the strongest living Processor's 15 percentage points, capped at 100%. Without living eligible mining equipment, a new root has zero recovery. Later damage, upgrades, refits and retries cannot reroll a root's extraction snapshot. Alien damage partitions the same finite budget.

Seismic Cut is the required Deep Extraction action, rather than an optional attack/defense ability. It uses the shared source picker, ground preview, Auto/manual commands, atomic cooldown persistence and deterministic replay. A visible rock cluster within 350 can receive 60 rock damage in radius 100; ships receive 15, with no fracture area-damage multiplier. Its fleet cooldown is 30 battle seconds. Unimplemented actions remain gated.

Mining Auto selects only visible, scanned, eligible rocks and skips known empty/locked deposits. Unknown visible rocks can prompt Scan. Mining ships approach tool range, slow alongside the rock and collect recoverable terminal pickups; escorts keep their hostile priority. Predictive short-range steering uses visible rocks only. Pickup approach eases off forward thrust and uses existing limited reverse thrust, avoiding an endless orbit around remaining debris. Both tool routes collect their complete finite root. The Drill fixture has redundant propulsion and completes with one small impact/engine loss rather than repeated collisions; physical damage can still strand an inadequately protected miner. Empty-field clearing and manual control are unnecessary.

Only terminal pickups pay. Per root/category, payment is the difference between the cumulative floor of processed ore times the frozen recovery rate and the already paid amount. Splitting cannot round every child upward. Nearby pickups are batched into one atomic checkpoint. The field's consumed identities, exact resulting inventory, physical losses, passenger/cargo losses, repairs and ability state commit together. A failed write grants nothing and leaves the pickup available, with automatic retry limited to once per simulation second. Pickup and settlement checkpoints preserve HP, motion, lineage, discoveries, extraction and claimed ore through interruption, withdrawal and retry; enemies can reset, resources cannot. Exact in-flight resume remains outside the existing checkpoint policy.

Automatic pickup write failures are external replay inputs. Replay consumes the recorded rejection at the same collection point after movement/collisions, preserving retry delay and event ordering without requiring a failing filesystem. Full-root mining, one rejected write followed by successful retry, and both authoritative state and event history match across batches of 1, 2 and 8 fixed ticks. These final checks are in `.local/enemy-roles/final/mining.log`.
