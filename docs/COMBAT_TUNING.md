# Baseline combat and repair tuning

Step 26 sets Laser to 3 DPS (12 damage every 4 seconds), Double Laser to 7.2 DPS (two 14.4-damage beams every 4 seconds), and the basic Shield to 40 capacity. Shields regenerate 4% of maximum capacity per second after four seconds without damage. A tick crossing that boundary recharges only for the portion after four seconds. Crew increases eligible firing/recharge rates without changing damage, capacity or interruption duration.

The shared movement calculation uses `r = thrust / (mass + thrust)`: top speed is `120 * sqrt(r)`, acceleration is `speed * (0.6 + 1.2*r)`, and turn rate is `0.75 + 0.75*r`. Zero thrust produces zero speed and acceleration. Crew multiplies acceleration/turning for affected engines, weighted by thrust; it does not increase top speed. Physical mass includes occupied material and equipment weight. Extra engines have diminishing returns, while larger armored hulls remain slower and retain larger world footprints. Armor HP and geometry are unchanged.

Construction, material purchase and reconstruction now share prices O/D/T/C = 5/2/8/6. Repair of surviving damaged material costs O/D/T/C = 1/0.2/3/2 per fully damaged tile, proportional to its component's missing HP. The total invoice rounds upward once. Partial damage retains the entire atomic unit and its materials.

Destroyed components require every material cell in their original footprint, including exhaust. Reconstruction consumes stored material first and automatically buys shortages if the whole quote is affordable. Repair and reconstruction are one operation on a surviving hull at a shipyard, blocked during battle and subject to current-run authorization. Fleet rows show the credit charge and indicate when stored materials are also required; the tooltip itemizes reconstruction and repair. Quotes refresh when inventory or credits change.

Both legacy and campaign dismantling paths call the same physical-hull operation. Only surviving material is returned; destroyed components never refund. Removing a host clears escort references and dedicated-slot ownership. A hull cannot refund twice. Repair HP, material consumption, credit payment, and dismantle removal/refunds are committed in one checkpoint before live state changes. Failed writes leave the live operation untouched. Reconstructed geometry invalidates its rendering cache once.

Existing blueprint/run formats and maximum component HP are unchanged. Existing saves retain their amounts and physical damage; derived stats and future purchase/repair prices use the new values. No currency conversion or retroactive refund occurs. Field repair equipment remains gated until its finite reserves and effects are implemented in step 27.

`tests/test_tuning.gd` covers weapon/shield values, mobility/armor tradeoffs, diminishing returns, exact shield timing, shared prices, invoice rounding, paid and inventory reconstruction, affordability, failed-save recovery, durable repair/dismantling, surviving-only refunds and render invalidation. It also checks the physical starter against three frozen first encounters (seeds 42, 1357 and 12345): all three ships survive each. The existing combat suite checks thrust, retreat, pursuit and 600 encounters; its synthetic fixture now specifies finite mass. The focused movement/balance run passed all 600, with damage min 2.02, mean 8.74, max 18.43. Native repair UI was inspected at 640×360 in `.local/tuning-repair.png`.
