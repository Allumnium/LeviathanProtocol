# LEVIATHAN PROTOCOL

> Narrative reference, not the current implementation backlog. The active slice and remaining D4/D5 work are in [TODO.md](../TODO.md); do not expand Act II/III, branches or endings without a scope decision.
## Narrative Design Specification — Version 1.2

**Genre:** Science-fiction expedition, fleet management, narrative roguelite  
**Structure:** Three acts, three principal endings  
**Themes:** Survival, autonomy, parental instinct, exploitation, institutional secrecy, and the ambiguity of good and evil.

---

# 1. Story Summary

The player commands a privately operated human flagship, leading a small commercial convoy through the frontier of colonized space.

The player chooses their company's specialization: combat, trade, passenger transportation, or general-purpose contracting.

Initially, the journey is ordinary. The player encounters merchants, civilian settlements, human pirates, corporate facilities, and military patrols.

Everything changes at a remote refueling station.

As the player's convoy prepares to depart, an unidentified biological organism emerges from space, attaches itself to the station, and begins spreading through its structure.

The station calls for assistance. The player can return and attempt to save it or continue traveling.

Shortly afterward, emergency broadcasts report a widespread biological invasion. A colossal alien organism has been detected in a neighboring sector.

The government activates **Leviathan Protocol**, a military emergency directive created following humanity's previous encounters with enormous biological organisms known as Leviathans.

All registered human flagships are ordered into military service.

The player joins a combined human fleet sent to confront the Leviathan.

The battle determines the central direction of the story.

A sufficiently powerful player can damage the Leviathan enough to force its retreat, remaining human and beginning a campaign to hunt it down.

A defeated player may instead survive through infestation. Alien organisms integrate themselves into the flagship, and the Leviathan offers to spare the player in exchange for retrieving its stolen offspring.

The player discovers that the alien attacks were provoked by the abduction of a developing Leviathan, contained within a chrysalis, by human contractors working for a powerful research corporation.

The corporation has been studying alien biology in hopes of developing advanced weapons, medical treatments, self-repairing vessels, and biological manufacturing.

Its discoveries could benefit humanity enormously, but its actions have also triggered a devastating conflict.

On the infested route, the player retrieves the chrysalis. During the journey back, it hatches and merges with the infested flagship, transforming the vessel into a fully developed new Leviathan.

Upon returning to its parent, the new Leviathan is recognized as an independent rival. The original demands biological dominance, while the player refuses to surrender control.

The two Leviathans fight to the death, with the surviving creature gaining dominance over the swarm.

Alternatively, a player who successfully resisted infestation can investigate the original abduction, pursue a military victory, or undertake the much more difficult task of negotiating peace.

The story has three principal endings:

1. Humanity defeats the Leviathan.
2. The player becomes a Leviathan and defeats the original.
3. Humanity and the aliens reach an uneasy negotiated peace.

Throughout the story, neither humanity nor the alien organisms are portrayed as entirely innocent or evil.

# 2. World and Historical Background

## 2.1 Human Civilization

Humanity occupies numerous connected star systems, ranging from heavily developed corporate sectors to remote, sparsely populated frontiers.

Civilian and commercial space travel is common.

Independent companies operate large flagships capable of transporting goods, carrying passengers, escorting convoys, conducting research, and engaging in combat.

The major political and economic powers include:

- Colonial governments and military authorities.
- Large corporations with extensive economic and political influence.
- Independent commercial companies.
- Frontier communities.
- Pirates and unregistered private fleets.

Human civilization is not unified politically.

Governments, corporations, military officers, and civilian operators frequently have competing interests.

## 2.2 Alien Biology

The alien organisms are an intelligent species adapted to living and traveling in space.

Their spacecraft are themselves living organisms.

They possess armored exoskeletons, biological propulsion, integrated weapons, regenerative tissues, and specialized organs.

Smaller organisms may operate independently or as parts of larger biological collectives.

The largest known members of the species are called Leviathans.

Leviathans can coordinate extensive swarms and communicate with subordinate organisms through biological signals that humanity does not fully understand.

Their intelligence is established, but their social organization, motivations, and individual identities remain poorly understood.

## 2.3 Previous Leviathan Encounters

The Leviathan encountered during the campaign is not the first known to humanity.

Historical records document previous encounters with organisms of similar size and biological characteristics.

Some encounters ended in military conflict.

Others ended when the organisms withdrew or disappeared.

Because encounters are rare, much of the available information remains speculative.

Military and corporate organizations maintain classified archives of these incidents.

Some previous Leviathans may have been destroyed. Others may remain alive somewhere beyond the settled regions.

The relationship between individual Leviathans is unknown.

## 2.4 Leviathan Protocol

Leviathan Protocol was established following earlier encounters with Leviathan-class organisms.

It is a standing emergency directive intended to coordinate humanity's response to biological threats of extraordinary scale.

When activated, it grants military authorities extensive emergency powers, including the right to requisition privately operated capital vessels.

The protocol's existence is publicly known, but many of its supporting intelligence reports are classified.

Its enforcement is controversial.

Military authorities regard the protocol as essential to defending human civilization.

Civilian operators criticize the compulsory deployment of commercial vessels into extremely dangerous engagements.

The current crisis marks one of the largest activations of the protocol in history.

## 2.5 The Stolen Chrysalis

Before the events of the campaign, a human research corporation authorized a covert mission to capture a developing alien organism.

The operation was carried out by pirates or private contractors.

The target was a chrysalis containing the developing offspring of the campaign's original Leviathan.

The corporation believed the chrysalis could provide unprecedented insight into alien biology and reproduction.

The operation was successful, and the chrysalis was transported to a secret research facility.

The original Leviathan subsequently began searching for its missing offspring.

Its search evolved into increasingly violent attacks against human installations and vessels.

The theft is the principal cause of the current crisis, although the alien response has expanded far beyond those directly responsible.

This fact is not known to the player at the beginning of the campaign.

# 3. Principal Factions and Motivations

## 3.1 The Player's Company

The player is the commander of an independent flagship and its supporting convoy.

The company may specialize in:

- Armed security and combat contracting.
- Commercial trading and cargo transportation.
- Passenger transportation.
- General-purpose commercial services.

The player begins without any special knowledge of the alien conflict.

The company is motivated primarily by commercial success, the safety of its vessels, and the survival of its personnel.

Its original identity should remain relevant to dialogue and how other characters perceive the player.

## 3.2 Colonial Military Authority

The military is responsible for defending human settlements.

Most officers genuinely believe that the Leviathan threatens human civilization.

Some are prepared to sacrifice civilian vessels if necessary to stop it.

Others oppose the scale of the forced mobilization.

Military leadership initially believes that destroying the Leviathan is the only reliable way to stop the invasion.

Later evidence of the stolen chrysalis creates disagreement within the military.

Some officers believe the new information changes nothing.

Others begin questioning whether diplomacy might be possible.

## 3.3 The Research Corporation

The corporation is responsible for commissioning the chrysalis abduction.

Its objectives include developing biological technologies that could provide humanity with enormous advantages.

Corporate research has already produced legitimate discoveries, including medical advances and systems designed to counter alien biological weapons.

The corporation believes that understanding Leviathans is necessary to protect humanity from future encounters.

However, its leadership knowingly authorized an extraordinarily dangerous operation and concealed its responsibility after the attacks began.

Some corporate employees defend the research program.

Others believe the chrysalis should be returned.

The corporation is not uniformly evil, but its leadership has strong incentives to conceal what happened.

## 3.4 The Original Leviathan

The original Leviathan is an ancient, intelligent organism searching for its abducted offspring.

Its motivation is genuine.

It has attacked human facilities in an effort to locate the chrysalis, identify the abductors, and recover its child.

However, its methods are violent and indiscriminate by human standards.

The Leviathan does not consistently distinguish between guilty individuals, corporate property, military installations, and civilian settlements.

It believes the recovery of its offspring takes precedence over human territorial claims and individual human lives.

The Leviathan is capable of communication and negotiation, but does not necessarily recognize human concepts of sovereignty or individual rights.

It should never be portrayed as secretly benevolent.

## 3.5 The Chrysalis

The chrysalis contains the developing offspring of the original Leviathan.

It is a living organism rather than a technological weapon.

The developing creature is capable of communicating through biological signals, although its awareness is initially limited.

The corporation regards the chrysalis as both a scientific specimen and a possible existential threat.

The original Leviathan regards it as its offspring.

On the infested storyline, the chrysalis eventually becomes the biological foundation of the player's transformation into a new Leviathan.

# 4. Act I — The Frontier

**Narrative theme:** Independence, ordinary life, and the intrusion of an incomprehensible threat.

## 4.1 Opening

The player begins a routine commercial expedition.

The journey establishes the atmosphere of human frontier civilization.

The player encounters stations, traders, passengers, corporate representatives, pirates, and civilian communities.

Story events should emphasize that the player's flagship is an independent commercial vessel rather than a military warship.

There should be no direct alien encounters before the refueling station incident.

## 4.2 Foreshadowing

Optional encounters provide subtle clues about unusual activity.

Examples include:

- A survey vessel that disappeared near restricted space.
- A private military convoy carrying unidentified cargo.
- A merchant who reports increased security around corporate facilities.
- A scientist discussing classified biological research.
- Unusual government interest in remote frontier sectors.

These clues should not immediately reveal the chrysalis theft.

Players who encounter them should recognize their significance later.

## 4.3 Port Meridian

The player visits a remote refueling and trading station called **Port Meridian**.

The station is an ordinary commercial outpost serving independent vessels.

The player refuels, trades, or interacts with station personnel.

As the flagship departs, an unidentified organic object approaches the station.

The organism attaches itself to the exterior docking ring and penetrates the station's hull.

Alien tissue begins spreading across the structure.

Smaller organisms emerge.

Station personnel issue a desperate distress call.

### Suggested Dialogue

**Port Meridian Control:**

"Unidentified contact approaching the outer docking ring."

"No transponder. No response to hails."

"Hold on. It's changing course."

"Something's attached itself to the station."

"We're losing pressure in three compartments. Internal security reports movement inside the walls."

"All departing vessels, please respond. We have civilians trapped aboard."

"Please. We need help."

## 4.4 Return or Depart

The player chooses whether to return and defend the station.

If they return, they confront the first small alien swarm.

If they succeed, they may rescue survivors and prevent the station's total destruction.

If they continue traveling, the station's fate is learned through later reports.

Neither choice prevents the main narrative from progressing.

The incident should establish the aliens as genuinely frightening and capable of rapid biological infestation.

## 4.5 The Emergency Broadcast

Reports of alien attacks begin arriving from multiple sectors.

Military communications reveal that a colossal biological entity has been detected.

The creature matches the classification of a Leviathan.

The government activates Leviathan Protocol.

### Mandatory Broadcast

**COLONIAL EMERGENCY BROADCAST SYSTEM**

"Attention all registered vessels."

"A Leviathan-class biological entity has been confirmed within the outer colonial sectors."

"Multiple civilian and military installations have reported coordinated attacks by organic spacecraft."

"Under the Colonial Defense Charter, **LEVIATHAN PROTOCOL IS NOW IN EFFECT**."

"All registered capital-class vessels are hereby placed under emergency military authority."

"Flagship commanders are ordered to report to their assigned assembly sectors."

"Failure to comply will be treated as a violation of emergency defense regulations."

"Further instructions will follow."

## 4.6 End of Act I

The player's commercial expedition is interrupted by military conscription.

The convoy travels toward a staging sector where the military is assembling a fleet to confront the Leviathan.

The player has no knowledge of the stolen chrysalis or the true cause of the attacks.

# 5. Act II — The Protocol

**Narrative theme:** Duty, military catastrophe, and conflicting explanations.

## 5.1 Military Assembly

The player arrives at a large military staging area.

Numerous military warships, civilian capital vessels, and support fleets are preparing for combat.

The atmosphere is tense.

Some commanders are confident that the combined force will destroy the Leviathan.

Others believe the operation is dangerously underprepared.

Several civilian captains protest their compulsory involvement.

Corporate representatives urge the military to protect particular installations, but offer limited explanations for their importance.

The player receives orders to participate in the interception operation.

## 5.2 First Leviathan Encounter

The combined fleet approaches the alien swarm.

The Leviathan appears as an enormous biological capital organism surrounded by smaller alien vessels.

Its size and destructive capabilities exceed most human expectations.

The human fleet engages.

Multiple ships are destroyed.

The player's flagship participates in the battle.

There are three possible narrative outcomes:

**Successful resistance:** The Leviathan suffers severe damage and retreats. The player remains human.

**Defeat and infestation:** The player's flagship is disabled and infested, but survives. The player enters the alien storyline.

**Destruction:** The player's flagship is destroyed and the run ends. This is an ordinary failure state, not a separate narrative ending.

## 5.3 A Critical Revelation

Both surviving storylines eventually reveal that the Leviathan's offensive is connected to a stolen chrysalis.

The circumstances of that discovery differ between the human and infested paths.

Human players uncover the information through military records, witnesses, corporate documents, and investigation.

Infested players learn the underlying motive through communication with the Leviathan, later corroborated by human evidence.

Neither explanation is initially complete.

# 6. The Human Storyline — The Hunt

## 6.1 After the Victory

The Leviathan retreats after taking significant damage.

Military commanders recognize the player's contribution.

The battle is declared a major human victory, although the threat has not been eliminated.

The player is assigned to help locate and destroy the wounded creature.

Public broadcasts celebrate the successful defense.

The player becomes a symbol of human resistance.

## 6.2 Pursuit

The Leviathan moves through remote sectors, leaving biological debris, damaged installations, and scattered alien organisms.

The player follows its movements.

Some human facilities along the pursuit route have been deliberately attacked.

Others appear untouched.

This pattern raises questions about the Leviathan's actual objectives.

## 6.3 Evidence of the Abduction

The player discovers classified records connecting the alien attacks to a covert human operation.

The documents identify a captured biological specimen transported to a corporate research facility.

Additional evidence suggests the specimen is the Leviathan's developing offspring.

The corporation commissioned the original capture.

The theft occurred before the first attacks of the current crisis.

## 6.4 The Military Disagreement

The evidence creates disagreement among human authorities.

One group argues that the Leviathan must be destroyed regardless of its motivation.

They point to destroyed settlements, dead civilians, and the continuing danger.

Another group argues that returning the chrysalis may provide a way to stop the attacks.

Military leadership is reluctant to trust the alien organism or acknowledge that human actions provoked the crisis.

The corporation warns that returning the specimen could lead to the development of another Leviathan.

The player must decide whether to continue the military hunt or pursue a diplomatic alternative.

## 6.5 The Branch

The human storyline separates into two possible conclusions:

**Military victory:** The player continues hunting the Leviathan and eventually destroys it.

**Diplomatic resolution:** The player investigates the chrysalis, establishes communication, and attempts to negotiate a settlement.

The diplomatic storyline is not an ordinary dialogue choice. It requires resolving several narrative conflicts that would otherwise make peace impossible.

---

# 7. The Infested Storyline — The Bargain

## 7.1 Defeat

The player's flagship is overwhelmed during the first Leviathan battle.

Alien organisms attach themselves to the vessel.

Biological tissue grows across external armor and penetrates internal machinery.

The ship becomes disabled.

Communications with human military forces are lost.

The crew remains alive, but the flagship is increasingly integrated with alien biological systems.

## 7.2 First Communication

The infestation begins communicating through the ship's systems.

Initially, the messages consist of distorted images, unusual sensor patterns, and fragmented impressions.

Eventually, the original Leviathan makes contact.

The Leviathan reveals that its offspring was stolen by humans.

It is attacking human facilities because it believes its offspring is being held somewhere within human-controlled space.

It cannot safely recover the chrysalis itself because the responsible corporation has developed sophisticated anti-biological defenses.

The player's partly human flagship offers an unusual opportunity.

## 7.3 The Ultimatum

The Leviathan offers to spare the player and their surviving crew.

In exchange, the player must retrieve the stolen chrysalis.

### Suggested Dialogue

**Leviathan:**

"You are not the ones who took it."

"But you came with them."

"You carry their machines. Their weapons."

"Our young is beyond our reach."

"It calls to us from behind walls that burn our flesh."

"You will enter where we cannot."

"You will return what was taken."

"Do this, and you may continue."

The player may accept or refuse.

Refusal ends the run as the Leviathan destroys or fully consumes the flagship.

Acceptance allows the player to continue.

## 7.4 The Hybrid Flagship

The infestation remains part of the vessel.

The flagship begins developing biological capabilities.

Human machinery and organic tissue coexist.

The alien organism integrated into the ship can regenerate damaged structures, develop weapons, and eventually produce smaller biological vessels.

The player retains command.

The crew's reactions are mixed.

Some regard the transformation as a necessary means of survival.

Others fear that the flagship is becoming something uncontrollable.

The Leviathan periodically communicates with the player, but provides only information relevant to recovering its offspring.

It is not a trustworthy companion.

## 7.5 Journey into Corporate Space

The player follows information provided by the Leviathan and evidence gathered from human sources.

The trail leads into heavily defended corporate sectors.

The corporation has developed advanced technologies specifically intended to suppress alien organisms.

The player discovers that the corporation's experiments have produced valuable medical and technological innovations.

They also uncover evidence of the chrysalis's abduction and mistreatment.

Some corporate researchers insist that returning the specimen would be catastrophic.

Others express regret and suggest that the corporation created the conditions for the current war.

## 7.6 Recovery of the Chrysalis

The player reaches the research facility and recovers the living chrysalis.

The organism is transported aboard the infested flagship.

The original Leviathan directs the player to return.

The player begins the journey back.

---

# 8. Act III — The Chrysalis and the Leviathan

**Narrative theme:** Identity, ownership, freedom, and the consequences of earlier choices.

Act III resolves the conflict through one of three principal narrative routes.

---

# 9. Route One — Human Victory

## 9.1 Final Pursuit

The player remains human.

They reject or abandon diplomatic efforts and continue pursuing the wounded Leviathan.

The creature attempts to recover, evade the human fleet, and protect the remaining swarm.

Military intelligence eventually identifies its location.

The player prepares for a final confrontation.

## 9.2 Final Battle

The player intercepts the original Leviathan.

Unlike the first encounter, the human fleet is better prepared and understands more of the creature's biology.

The Leviathan refuses to retreat or surrender.

The final battle ends with its destruction.

The remaining swarm becomes disorganized and withdraws or disperses.

## 9.3 Ending — Humanity's Champion

The player returns to human territory as a celebrated military hero.

The invasion has been stopped.

The player's company receives official recognition.

However, the truth of the chrysalis abduction may complicate the celebration.

If the player uncovered the corporation's involvement, its actions may become the subject of public controversy or investigation.

Humanity has survived, but the possibility of future encounters with other Leviathans remains.

The final tone should be victorious but not entirely triumphant.

The player has destroyed an organism responsible for widespread devastation, but also a parent attempting to recover its stolen child.

---

# 10. Route Two — Leviathan Ascendancy

## 10.1 The Return Journey

After recovering the chrysalis, the infested flagship begins traveling toward the original Leviathan.

During the journey, the chrysalis exhibits increasingly intense biological activity.

The ship's existing infestation responds.

The organism eventually hatches.

## 10.2 The Merger

The newly hatched Leviathan offspring bonds with the alien biological tissue integrated into the flagship.

The process becomes irreversible.

The organism incorporates the vessel into its developing body.

Human machinery merges with specialized biological organs.

The ship develops a massive armored exoskeleton.

Its propulsion, weapons, and structural systems become increasingly biological.

The transformation is progressive.

The player's original flagship gradually becomes a new organism of extraordinary size and power.

By the end of the transformation, the ship has become a **fully developed Leviathan**.

The player's command systems remain integrated with the organism.

The player retains operational control, although it is unclear whether the new Leviathan's consciousness is entirely separate from the commander.

The transformation is not a partial infestation.

The original human flagship has effectively become the body of a new Leviathan.

## 10.3 The Parent Recognizes Its Offspring

The player returns to the original Leviathan.

The original recognizes the biological identity of its child.

However, the offspring has become a fully developed, independent Leviathan, integrated with human technology and command systems.

The parent is initially confused or distressed by what it encounters.

The new organism is unmistakably its offspring, but it is no longer the dependent creature the parent expected to recover.

The original Leviathan attempts to assert biological dominance.

It demands that the new organism submit to the existing swarm hierarchy.

The player's independence conflicts with the parent's instinct to control its offspring and maintain dominance over the collective.

This conflict cannot be resolved through ordinary negotiation.

The original Leviathan perceives another fully developed Leviathan as a potential rival.

The player, now commanding a complete Leviathan, cannot simply return to their former life or surrender the new organism's autonomy.

## 10.4 The Final Ultimatum

### Suggested Dialogue

**Original Leviathan:**

"You carry our young."

"No."

"You are our young."

"You have grown beyond your place."

"You will return to us."

"You will yield."

The player refuses to surrender command.

The original Leviathan interprets this refusal as a challenge to its dominance.

Both organisms prepare for battle.

## 10.5 Battle of the Leviathans

The player fights the original Leviathan.

Both are colossal biological organisms capable of producing and commanding swarms.

The original possesses experience and an established biological collective.

The player possesses a newly developed Leviathan with unusual human-alien hybrid characteristics.

Neither can retreat permanently from the conflict.

The confrontation continues until one Leviathan is destroyed.

## 10.6 Ending — Leviathan Ascendancy

The player defeats and kills the original Leviathan.

The surviving alien swarm responds to the death of its former dominant organism.

The new Leviathan becomes the swarm's central authority.

The player now commands a vast biological force.

The organism they once feared has become their own body and source of power.

Human civilization recognizes the emergence of a new Leviathan-class entity.

Some authorities prepare for another war.

Others wonder whether the new Leviathan, with its human origins, might behave differently from its predecessor.

The ending should emphasize an extraordinary transformation without defining the player as a hero or villain.

The player has survived, gained enormous power, and replaced the original Leviathan.

What they do with that power remains uncertain.

---

# 11. Route Three — An Uneasy Peace

## Narrative Principle

The peace ending must feel difficult, uncertain, and genuinely earned.

It is not achieved by simply selecting a diplomatic dialogue option.

The player must uncover the truth, establish credible communication, address the stolen chrysalis, and prevent both sides from destroying one another before negotiations can succeed.

**For the initial narrative, this route branches from the human storyline.**

A player who survives the first Leviathan battle without infestation can pursue diplomacy.

A player who accepts infestation follows the biological transformation storyline, which culminates in the battle between Leviathans.

This distinction keeps the routes clear and preserves the inevitability of the final confrontation once the chrysalis merges with the flagship.

## 11.1 Discovering the Truth

During the hunt for the wounded Leviathan, the player discovers evidence that the corporation commissioned the chrysalis theft.

The evidence includes transport records, research documents, and testimony from individuals involved in the operation.

The player learns that the Leviathan's current attacks began after the abduction.

The connection does not excuse the alien attacks, but it provides a plausible explanation for the creature's behavior.

## 11.2 Establishing Communication

The player discovers research suggesting that Leviathan biological signals can be interpreted.

Earlier human encounters produced incomplete translation systems, but the military never successfully established sustained communication.

The player locates specialists or surviving research records capable of interpreting the original Leviathan's signals.

The first attempts are unsuccessful.

The Leviathan sees approaching human vessels as potential threats and does not immediately respond.

The player must demonstrate nonhostile intentions while avoiding actions the creature would interpret as another attack.

Eventually, limited communication becomes possible.

## 11.3 The Leviathan's Demands

The original Leviathan confirms that its offspring was taken.

It demands the immediate return of the chrysalis.

It also demands an end to human interference with its territory and developing organisms.

It does not offer an unconditional ceasefire.

The player learns that the Leviathan does not necessarily distinguish between the corporation responsible for the theft and humanity as a whole.

The player must explain that humans are not a unified collective and that many human settlements had no involvement.

The Leviathan initially finds this distinction difficult to understand.

## 11.4 The Human Opposition

Human military leaders are skeptical of the player's claims.

Many regard the Leviathan as too dangerous to negotiate with.

The corporation strongly opposes returning the chrysalis.

Corporate representatives argue that allowing another Leviathan to mature could threaten the survival of human civilization.

They also warn that the chrysalis may have been altered by human experimentation.

The player must persuade at least some military and civilian authorities that a controlled exchange is preferable to continued war.

Not everyone agrees.

## 11.5 Securing the Chrysalis

The player must gain possession of the chrysalis without allowing it to hatch aboard an infested vessel.

Because the player's flagship remains human, the chrysalis does not merge with it.

The corporation may refuse to release the specimen.

The player may have to confront corporate security, expose the research program, or obtain support from sympathetic scientists.

The recovery should reveal that the chrysalis has been subjected to invasive experimentation.

However, its survival is confirmed.

## 11.6 A Demonstration of Good Faith

Before agreeing to an exchange, both sides demand evidence that the other will honor an agreement.

The player arranges a limited ceasefire near a neutral location.

The military agrees to suspend operations against a designated portion of the swarm.

The Leviathan is asked to withdraw its organisms from threatened civilian installations.

This creates a significant moment of uncertainty.

The player must trust an alien intelligence that has already destroyed human settlements.

The Leviathan must trust representatives of a species that abducted its offspring.

A hostile incident or deliberate sabotage threatens to collapse the arrangement.

The player discovers that elements within the corporation or military may prefer renewed conflict to the loss of strategic advantage.

The player must prevent the ceasefire from collapsing.

## 11.7 The Exchange

The chrysalis is transported to a neutral location under the supervision of the player and cooperating human representatives.

The original Leviathan approaches.

It demands the release of its offspring.

The player insists that the Leviathan withdraw its swarm and cease attacks against human settlements.

Communication is difficult.

Neither side fully understands the other's concepts of promises, territory, and responsibility.

Eventually, the Leviathan accepts the exchange.

The chrysalis is returned before hatching.

The original Leviathan recognizes its offspring and withdraws with it.

## 11.8 Ending — An Uneasy Peace

The immediate invasion ends.

A provisional agreement establishes boundaries between human-controlled territory and regions claimed by the Leviathan.

Human authorities agree to restrictions on the capture and experimentation of alien organisms.

The Leviathan agrees to cease attacks on human settlements and withdraw its swarm.

The corporation's actions become public, although its leadership disputes aspects of the account.

The player is recognized by some as the individual who prevented an interspecies war.

Others condemn the agreement as a dangerous concession to an existential threat.

There is no guarantee the peace will last.

Humanity still possesses advanced anti-alien weapons.

The Leviathan still commands a powerful biological swarm.

Other Leviathans may exist.

The chrysalis may eventually grow into another massive organism.

The ending should be hopeful without claiming that the conflict has been permanently resolved.

## Act I — The Frontier

- Company specialization establishes their identity.
- The player encounters ordinary frontier society.
- An alien organism attacks Port Meridian.
- Player may return to help or continue.
- The invasion is announced.
- Leviathan Protocol is activated.
- The player is conscripted.

## Act II — The Protocol

- The player joins the combined human fleet.
- The original Leviathan is confronted.
- The player's performance determines whether they remain human or become infested.
- Both surviving paths uncover the truth of the stolen chrysalis.

### Human Branch
- Pursue the wounded Leviathan.
- Discover corporate involvement.
- Decide whether to continue the hunt or investigate diplomacy.

### Infested Branch
- Survive through infestation.
- Receive the Leviathan's ultimatum.
- Accept the chrysalis recovery mission.
- Travel into hostile corporate sectors.
- Retrieve the chrysalis.

### Human Victory
- Locate the Leviathan.
- Destroy it.
- Return as a celebrated human commander.

### Leviathan Ascendancy
- Chrysalis hatches aboard the flagship.
- Chrysalis merges with the infestation.
- Flagship becomes a complete new Leviathan.
- Return to the original Leviathan.
- Refuse biological submission.
- Fight the original to the death.
- Become dominant over the swarm.

- Remain human.
- Prove the chrysalis was stolen.

- Recover the chrysalis.
- Overcome military and corporate opposition.
- Arrange a ceasefire and exchange.
- Return the offspring before it hatches.
- Establish a provisional peace.

# 13. Narrative Rules

## Rule 1: No Simple Good or Evil

No faction should be portrayed as unquestionably morally superior.

Humanity has committed exploitation and abduction, but is also defending settlements from catastrophic attacks.

The Leviathan is genuinely attempting to recover its offspring, but its methods cause extensive destruction.

## Rule 2: The Leviathan's Motivation Is Genuine

The original Leviathan is truly searching for its stolen child.

This revelation is not a deception.

However, the creature may also be territorial, authoritarian, and willing to use extreme violence.

## Rule 3: The Corporation Has Mixed Motivations

The corporation caused the immediate crisis through the chrysalis theft.

Its leadership knowingly accepted substantial risks.

However, its research has also produced valuable advances and may genuinely improve humanity's chances of surviving future alien encounters.

## Rule 4: The Human Path Is Valid

The player can complete the campaign without infestation.

Successfully resisting the original Leviathan should lead to a complete and satisfying storyline.

The human victory must not be treated as an inferior or incorrect ending.

## Rule 5: Infestation Leads to Full Transformation

On the infested storyline, recovery of the chrysalis leads to its hatching and merger with the flagship.

The flagship becomes a fully developed new Leviathan.

There is no intermediate ending involving partial transformation or voluntary surrender.

## Rule 6: The Leviathan Confrontation Is Unavoidable

Once the player has become a new Leviathan and returns to the original, the two organisms enter a dominance conflict.

The player must fight the original Leviathan to the death.

The outcome is victory or defeat, not assimilation.

## Rule 7: Peace Must Be Earned

The peace ending requires substantial prior investigation and several meaningful diplomatic developments.

The player must address the underlying causes of the conflict.

Neither faction simply changes its mind because the player requests peace.

## Rule 8: Narrative Uncertainty Remains

Not every historical mystery should be resolved.

The game should not definitively establish whether humanity or the aliens represent the greater long-term danger.

The player should understand why each faction acts as it does without necessarily approving of those actions.

## Rule 9: Preserve the Player's Company Identity

The player began as an independent civilian commander.

That identity should remain relevant.

The story is not about a chosen military hero, but an ordinary commercial operator forced into an extraordinary conflict.

## Rule 10: Tone and Dialogue

Use grounded science-fiction dialogue.

Military communications should sound professional and bureaucratic.

Corporate dialogue should sound pragmatic, defensive, and technically informed.

Civilian dialogue should convey the personal consequences of major events.

The Leviathan should communicate in short, unfamiliar constructions that suggest an intelligence with concepts different from humanity's.

Avoid caricatured villains, exaggerated speeches, and explicit moral judgments.

---

# 14. Important Narrative Dependencies

The following relationships must remain consistent throughout the story.

**The chrysalis theft predates the invasion.** The original Leviathan's offensive is a response to its offspring being taken.

**Leviathan Protocol predates the invasion.** It was established after earlier Leviathan encounters.

**The first Leviathan battle determines the player's principal storyline.** Successful resistance leaves the player human; defeat may result in infestation.

**The human storyline divides into military victory or diplomacy.** Both are complete routes.

**Infestation creates the possibility of biological ship evolution.** It does not immediately transform the flagship into a Leviathan.

**The chrysalis is necessary for full transformation.** Its hatching and merger with the infested flagship create the new Leviathan.

**The peace route requires the chrysalis to be returned before it hatches and merges with an infested flagship.**

**The original Leviathan recognizes the transformed player as its offspring.** Its attempt to dominate the new organism causes the final battle.

**There are exactly three principal victory endings:** Human Victory, Leviathan Ascendancy, and Uneasy Peace.

Ordinary defeat remains possible throughout the campaign, but it is not treated as a separate authored narrative route.

---

# 15. Intentional Mysteries

The following questions should remain partially or entirely unresolved:

- How many Leviathans exist?
- What happened during humanity's earliest encounters with them?
- Are Leviathans naturally hostile to other fully developed Leviathans, or does their behavior depend on circumstances?
- How much individual consciousness exists within the alien collective?
- Does the chrysalis recognize human attempts to communicate with it?
- Did the corporation's research prevent future catastrophes, or create new ones?
- Would the original Leviathan have attacked humanity eventually even without the theft?
- Can the new hybrid Leviathan preserve its human perspective indefinitely?
- Will the negotiated peace survive another encounter with a different Leviathan?

These questions should encourage interpretation rather than demand definitive answers.

---

# 16. Final Narrative Identity

*Leviathan Protocol* begins as the story of a small commercial expedition caught in an unexpected war.

It becomes a story about institutional power, biological exploitation, and an alien parent's violent attempt to recover its child.

Depending on the player's path, it ends with one of three fundamentally different outcomes.

**Humanity's Champion:** The commander defeats the alien threat while remaining human.

**Leviathan Ascendancy:** The commander's flagship becomes the offspring of the original Leviathan, then kills its parent and inherits the swarm.

**An Uneasy Peace:** The commander uncovers the conflict's origins and negotiates a precarious settlement between two civilizations that barely understand one another.

None of these endings should be labeled the morally correct outcome.

The purpose of the narrative is to make each resolution understandable, consequential, and imperfect.
