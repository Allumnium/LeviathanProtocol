# Current ship-model direction (October 31, 2026)

The owner replaced both grid designs with image-based chassis and fixed equipment slots. No hexes, squares, placed materials or equipment recipes in the new Designer/game model. Each visible slot has a dropdown and a type (Offense, Defense, Tech or Civic, based on former equipment categories) plus a size (S/M/L/XL). Equipment must match the slot size EXACTLY: smaller equipment cannot fill larger slots. Equipment must also match the type and current research. Starting tech is available; research unlocks more. Preserve the simple direct-selection flow, minimal text, Starhull theme, no hover tooltips and no keyboard focus navigation. Source art: assets/corsair.png, fighter.png, transport.png, frigate.png, drone.png. Initial positions/types/sizes are editable draft data in data/slot_chassis.json. Slot size metadata is independent of discarded material recipes.

Main-menu scenes/ship_designer.tscn now uses slot_ship_designer.gd. Saved loadouts use separate user://slot_designs.json, format chassis_slots_v1/version 3. Square/hex archives must remain readable and must not be silently converted. The prior hex Designer is archived at scenes/hex_ship_designer.tscn for compatibility tests, not linked from the main menu. Existing campaign GameState, economy, battle and checkpoints still need the explicit slot migration; do not claim they are already switched. Use credits for the new model's costs instead of construction materials. No further hex/recipe development. Retain previously confirmed mandatory flagship, command budget and no-reserve rules unless the owner changes them. See docs/SLOT_DESIGNER.md and TODO.md for next required work.

# User interface preferences

These are explicit preferences from the project owner. Preserve them in future UI work:

- Keep the Starhull font. Render bitmap text at crisp pixel sizes; fix layout and scaling rather than substituting a different body font.
- Avoid hover tooltips. The conditional lock explanation over Ship Designer is the exception the user accepts. Show necessary prices, restrictions and feedback inline or in a panel opened by clicking.
- The hex/chassis fleet has no design-slot limit: one flagship plus other ships/designs. Replace old act-based deployment capacity with command points supplied by the flagship chassis; non-flagship chassis have command costs. No dedicated Science/Civic slots, offense limits or equipment segregation. Earlier three-slot/act-capacity rules apply only to the legacy square model during migration.
- No Port or Story buttons, service-explanation prose or automatic story popups in the game UI. Preserve underlying arrival/repair/research/story progression. The top bar shows resources and level, without Act/row or duplicate ship-count/hull/damage statistics; fleet condition belongs in the fleet summary.
- Put actions for an individual physical ship beside that ship in the formation view, only while exactly one ship is selected. Do not repeat per-ship actions in the fleet summary. The hex fleet has no reserve/deploy toggle.
- No Tab or arrow-key cycling between UI controls, and no focus-outline boxes on buttons, tabs, sliders or texture controls. Text fields still accept typing and canvas/gameplay shortcuts remain available.
- UI fonts and reusable component styles belong in `themes/ui_theme.tres`; use theme inheritance/type variations instead of per-control font resources, fallback fonts or copied style boxes. Changing the theme's default font should update the UI.
- No reserve ships in the hex/chassis model. Every owned ship deploys and non-flagships consume command points. Remove reserve state/actions/modal during migration; do not add another inactive-ship category. Preserve legacy saves until the explicit transition is ready.
- Shop slots have no minus/salvage button. Salvage the singly selected physical hull through its formation actions immediately, without confirmation. Construction opens a plural quantity-order window: preview/base sprite costs/equipment on the left, actual order breakdown on the right, shared top-right X and centered Construct button; no large Cancel button.
- Do not add unrequested features while implementing remaining milestones. Ask before adding a feature outside the requested scope.
- Do not offer individual construction-material purchases. Construction/repair and other material-consuming actions use owned materials first, then charge credits for shortages at the existing material exchange rates. Credits act as material wild cards; this is separate from the planned cargo-goods trade.

- Research is one pannable/zoomable tree with categories as major branches, never category filters. Omit Foundation entirely from the UI; its knowledge is assumed. Keep research text minimal: names, unlocks, essential numbers and Set Focus. Completed nodes show a drawn green check above the name; partial nodes show current / required RP above the name. The left panel shows production and planned arrival yield, not categories. Each living deployed lab produces 1 RP/day, plus a fleet baseline of 0.5 RP/day (including no labs); multiply by planned travel days, typically 2-4 per route. Preserve fractional RP, focus and partial projects across saves. Freeze journey time/labs at departure and settle once on completed travel; battle duration never farms RP. Only the header shows the unspent RP balance. No View Grants, node modals, bad disclosure glyphs, next labels or instant-purchase UI.

# Superseded hex prototype (October 30, 2026)

Hex equipment has no functional orientation. Recognize all six rotations and reflections as the same recipe and behavior. Footprint transforms are placement metadata only: weapons aim automatically and thrusters contribute ship-wide thrust. Do not add facing arrows, directional placement requirements, or equipment Rotate/Flip controls, including in Archive. Keep group names upright and use the shared theme's default font.

The next required work is the hex/chassis rework before TODO step 56. See `docs/HEX_CHASSIS_REWORK.md` for confirmed rules and unresolved design details. Ships use fixed connected hex chassis, material recipes and designated construction regions only. Regions have at least one intervening hex gap and each component must fit entirely within one region. Every chassis includes a command center; built-in equipment has fixed placement and can be destroyed/repaired. The chassis has one integrity pool and breaks as a whole below zero, not at zero. Equipment hits split damage equally between component and chassis; bare-chassis hits damage the chassis. Start with five chassis including larger flagship chassis. Require exactly one physical flagship, deployed every battle; its destruction (including command-center loss) ends the run. Flagship replacement must be atomic at a shipyard. No limited design slots, act-based deployment capacity or reserves: flagship chassis provide command points; other owned/deployed chassis consume them. These decisions supersede earlier slot/capacity/reserve and singleton rules; science/civic specialization remains player-chosen. Preserve existing saves and blueprints through an explicit versioned transition. Chassis acquisition/unlock systems have not been specified.

The main-menu Designer now edits hex chassis through native hexagonal TileMapLayers. Material art belongs in themes/hex_material_palette.tres; generated hexes are valid placeholders until the owner finishes artwork. Hex designs use their own atomic user://hex_designs.json library. Preserve legacy archives and the embedded campaign builder until combat/refit migration is ready. Do not silently convert square saves or unlock research from creative design. See docs/HEX_CHASSIS_REWORK.md for current integration status.

For hex equipment, do not reuse the legacy square equipment sprites. Draw the actual combined group's outline and center its name on it, using the shared theme font; the owner will supply new equipment art later.
